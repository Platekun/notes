# The Illusion That Time Has Stopped

Robotic;Notes’ protagonist mentioned this when talking about the future he was going to face soon after he graduates from high school. He made no effort on preparing for that future, instead he concentrated on playing Kill-Ballad (A fighting game).

I have felt like this many times:

- When the pandemic occurred and everybody had to work from home I felt like “*Time has stopped for me*“ and “*My life stopped for two years*”.
- When working with horrible clients at my job.
- When I do not engage on other activities beside work in my life.

I think this kind of thinking is dreadful. It usually appears in my mind when I am in a negative situation. Maybe this is just a wishful thinking in order to make my boredom bearable, kind of like trying trying to toggle an “automatic mode” in real life. Is this the moment I should consider that my life is not the way I want it to be and I should try to see what I can work on so I can be happier? I don’t know. I am scared and tired of taking a bad decision.

“*It’s a pain in the ass to try and think about what comes*” 100% agree.

> *So, it’s entirely true I’ve done no prep when it comes to what I wanna do after school.

I blame it on the illusion that times has stopped on the island. It just makes you forget about the future entirely.

You end up thinking that things’ll just go on like this forever.

And I don’t mean that in the sense that I hope these perfect days will never end or whatever.

When time stops, so too does your ability to think.

And yet even when you realize this is the case, it’s still a pain in the ass to try and think about what comes next.

In the end…

I don’t care what happens, as long as I can keep gaming.

That’s how I continue putting everything off.*
