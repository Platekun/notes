# Truth and Illusion in Communication

Our daily dosis of schizophrenia is here! Jokes aside, this sets an interesting topic about world perception as we know it. With the rise of technologies as voice synthesisers it is somewhat possible to use a computer to perform a phone call using the voice of somebody else (Gotta test that). I also believe this is one of the reasons not to receive phone calls from strangers and only perform them myself, I immediately reduce the risk of a scam.

I understood the physics that let you talk to somebody far away via phone, but I still found it hard to believe.

When you talked on the phone, your vocie was turned into an electronic signal. This signal was transmitted down a wire to the person on the other end, and then turned back into a voice so they could be heard.

In other words, if you wanted to tak to somebody on the phone, your voice needed to be transformed a minimum of two times.

For a cellphone, first the electrical signals created from your vocie were sent to a nearby cell tower then transferred to a switching center via normal telephone lines. From there, they were sent to the tower nearest to the person you were talking to, and then sent via electromagnetic waves to their phone. It was an incredibly complicated and bizarre process.

If my voice was being transformed into electrical signals, could the person I was talking to really hear every single thing I was saying , exactly the way I said it?

Wasn't there a chance that in the process of turning it into an electrical signal, it could end up turned into something else altogether?

For instance, say I caleld Nanami, and instead of my voice being sent directly to her, it was sent to some weird building somewhere.

And there, somebody I'd never met could pretend to be Nanami.

Maybe I wasn't even talking to a person. I could be talking to a computer.

That's why talking on the phone was so scary to me. I was scarier that even talking to someone in person.*
