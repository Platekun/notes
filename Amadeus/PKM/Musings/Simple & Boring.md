# Simple & Boring

- Nobody is expecting you to do something complex.
- Everybody wants things to be simple, and everything starts simple by default.
- Web development has not gotten any simpler over the years. Sometimes the way developers add complexity gets in the way of getting work done.
- “*Simplicity*" can often mean "*Let the browser do things for you*" which helps makes your workload more bearable.
- The best example of simplicity is just starting with HTML. No CSS or JavaScript. Just learning and relying on HTML can help you a lot along the way, it won't be beautiful but things will work.
- The sudden interest in static site generators may be a symptom of this industry for yearning this simplicity.
- Strive to do as little as possible, advocate for consistency and approach differentiation carefully. ([https://alistapart.com/column/do-as-little-as-possible/](https://alistapart.com/column/do-as-little-as-possible/)).
- Developers need to take ownership for the complexity they add to the Web [https://ctmiller.net/blog/2019/01/25/a_simpler_web.html](https://ctmiller.net/blog/2019/01/25/a_simpler_web.html).
- All complexity is opt-in, it is not necessary.
- New technologies attract because make you feel you are doing your job well while nobody speaks about old technologies which makes you feel left out.
- The nice thing about boringness is that the capabilities are well understood. ([https://mcfunley.com/choose-boring-technology](https://mcfunley.com/choose-boring-technology)).
- Chatter does not equal to being better. That's why it is wiser to look at older alternatives.
- Developers are supposed to be complexity managers.
- Sandy Metz mentioned that "*complex code*" usually involves some factors, where one of them is that such code "represents core concepts in the domain" ([https://www.sandimetz.com/blog/2017/9/13/breaking-up-the-behemoth](https://www.sandimetz.com/blog/2017/9/13/breaking-up-the-behemoth)).
