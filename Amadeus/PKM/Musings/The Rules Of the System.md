# The Rules Of the System

[[Unconventional Thinking|Unconventional thinking]] is only part of an equation when it comes to [solving problems](https://app.notion.com/p/fc82447d37384ef79b2eff0ca9dd16ba?pvs=21). The other part is understanding the existing rules of the system. Those rules can be thought as the output of valuable experience which other people have in high regard. To be a effective professional, we need to understand how to balance these two. We need to understand when we should break our own rules.

The last sentence is easier said than done though, in life we will face with problems. That will make us question if we are making the right choices. It seems that to be a proper right-sider, you need to trust your instinct with the available information at hand.

On second thought: What I did on Walmart feels similar to what Shinjo did there. He didn't think conventionally but instead solved the case in an unorthodox way just like I solved my blockers.

> *[[Takeshi Shinjo]] : …

He didn't have a plan to follow. This was the first time that had happened, he thought. He only managed to be successful at his age around what Momose called the “hardheads” in Section 1 was that he knew how to be flexible.

The reason that many police officers could be stubborn and hard-headed was that most of the time, that was how you solved a case. Shinjo understood that the rules they clung to so fiercely, both written and unwritten, were the product of valuable experience. But he also understood that the unorthodox methods his [[Ban Yasuji|senior detective friend]] had taught him were a weapon in their own right. Even if the higher-ups didn’t like using them, they’d brought him success.

One good example was the time the profilers had told them that the person behind a string of assaults was a man in his twenties who never left his house. He'd found out that the culprit was actually a runaway girl in her late teens who "lived” in a net café. He'd matched the internet logs that Section 1 had gathered with the data held by the Societal Safety Section, a group that was on notoriously bad terms with Section 1. In the end, he'd cracked the case by logging onto an online game and tracking down several of its players, including the girl behind the attacks.

Shinjo was, if nothing else, confident that he was capable of not letting preconceptions keep him from making the best choice. But this time, not only did he not know what was the best choice, he didn’t even know if the choices he was making were good or bad.*
