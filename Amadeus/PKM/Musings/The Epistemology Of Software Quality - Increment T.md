# The Epistemology Of Software Quality - Increment: Teams

- *"What policies can I implement to make people more productive and code less buggy?"* It's a hard question to answer.
- People tend to focus too much on the technical side of the issue: The programming language, using visual solutions like UML or any other diagrams standard, micro-services, other techniques sold in books and conference, etc. We have to ask ourselves *"Do they even work at all?"*.
- We have too many *"experts"* selling technical answers to a question that may have some answers on the social side of things.
- Since *popular* is not the same as *good*, it's wise to do research on studies made by institutions and contrast those results against what you are being sold. Studies are what can establish some truths against folklore.
- There have been studies done on technical things like type systems, programming languages, pair programming, etc. that have not being conclusive about what those "experts" mention in their sale.
- However there have been more studies about how factors like stress, sleep and work-life balance affects productivity in workplaces.
- In our daily live we have more things that pushes us to be stressed and sleep-deprived, we don't have something that restrain us from doing that.
- Things that cause these situations are usually things outside of our control like being understaffed, the work culture, the scope creep of the project and in the long term it ends up hurting our health: *"It's easier to ask more of people than to address the sickness in the system"*.
