# “How does the Proof-of-stake work?” Prompt

> *In proof-of-stake (PoS) systems, the creator of a new block is chosen in a deterministic way, depending on their stake in the network. Stake, in this context, refers to the number of coins a miner holds.

Here's how it works:

Miners (called "validators" in PoS systems) are required to lock up a certain number of coins as stake.

When a new block is to be added to the blockchain, the validators are chosen to create the block in a deterministic way, depending on their stake. The higher the stake, the higher the chances of being chosen to create the next block.

The validator who creates the block is rewarded with a portion of the transaction fees from the transactions included in the block.

If a validator attempts to cheat or validate an invalid block, they risk losing their stake. This creates an economic disincentive for validators to act dishonestly and helps to ensure the security and integrity of the blockchain.

PoS systems are an alternative to proof-of-work (PoW) systems, which are used by cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin. In PoW systems, miners compete to solve a complex mathematical problem and the first one to solve it gets to create the next block and receive the reward. PoS systems are believed to be more energy efficient than PoW systems, as they do not require miners to perform computationally intensive tasks.*
